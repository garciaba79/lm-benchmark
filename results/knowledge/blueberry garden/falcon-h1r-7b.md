3. **Avoid overhead watering**: If you must water with a hose for spot irrigation, aim the stream at soil surface only.

### Why Ammonium Sulfate Is Ideal Now  

- **Acidic**: The sulfate adds extra acidity, helping maintain the low pH you’ve created.  
- **Quick uptake**: N from ammonium is taken up within a day or two—critical for early leaf expansion before buds swell.  

### What NOT to Use at This Stage  

- High‑P or high‑K synthetic fertilizers (e.g., super phosphate, potassium sulfate). Berries need more nitrogen and less excess potassium in the vegetative stage; too much K can cause fruit cracking later on.  
- Excessive organic N sources like fresh manure—manure is high in salts and can raise pH if not aged, plus it may introduce pathogens.

## 6. Quick FAQ Cheat‑Sheet  

- **How much water per week?** In dry weather aim for ≈ 30‑40 gal/plant total weekly, delivered as deep soak(s) via drip. Adjust down when rain supplies ≥ 1 in of moisture.  
- **Can I use coffee grounds or compost tea?** Both are fine in small amounts but avoid high nitrogen “green” teas that can scorch roots; stick to low‑N organic sources like pine needles or aged compost.  
- **What if a bird tears the net?** Repair within hours—use garden twine or zip ties to resecure mesh; you can also add temporary reflective tape around the damaged area as a deterrent while you replace it.  
- **Do I need micronutrients?** Most blueberries are fine on their own, but if soil test shows deficiencies in zinc or boron, apply a dilute chelated micronutrient spray at label rate once during fruit set.
