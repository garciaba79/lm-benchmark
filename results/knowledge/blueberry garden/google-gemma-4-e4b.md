Here is some comprehensive advice broken down by your concerns:

### General Improvement Tips

1.  **Pruning (Future Focus):** While you are establishing the bushes, in future years (especially as they become more established), plan to prune them lightly. Blueberry plants generally benefit from being pruned back slightly in late winter/early spring to encourage air circulation and promote vigorous new fruiting wood.
2.  **Soil Monitoring:** Stick closely to monitoring your pH levels. Because blueberries require acidic soil, monitor it annually. If the pH starts creeping up, you may need to apply elemental sulfur (following product instructions carefully) to help bring it back into the optimal 4.5–5.5 range.

### Pest and Disease Watch List

Keep a close eye on these common issues:

**Pests:**
*   **Aphids:** You handled this well with neem oil. Continue scouting regularly, as they can return quickly.
*   **Blueberry Moth/Borer:** Look for signs of boring tunnels or webbing on the leaves and developing berries. Neem oil is often effective here too.
*   **Spider Mites:** These are more common in hot, dry conditions. Look for fine webbing on the undersides of the leaves. Increasing humidity (through proper watering) can help deter them.

**Diseases:**
*   **Powdery Mildew:** This fuzzy white or grayish growth can appear on the leaves, especially when humidity is high and air circulation is poor. Improving spacing and ensuring good airflow through pruning are key preventative steps.
*   **Leaf Spot Diseases:** These appear as spots (brown or yellow) on the leaves. Proper air circulation and avoiding overhead watering are your best defenses.

### Water Management

Since you have a drip irrigation system, you are already ahead of the game! Here is how to maximize its effectiveness:

1.  **Deep and Infrequent Watering:** The goal is to encourage deep root growth, not just surface-level moisture. When you water, let it soak deeply into the root zone so that the roots have to work hard to find enough water.
2.  **Mulch Maintenance:** Keep that pine bark mulch in place! It acts as a vital barrier, keeping the soil temperature stable and drastically reducing the amount of water that evaporates during hot spells. Top it up annually if it washes away or breaks down too much.
3.  **Monitor Drainage:** Ensure your raised beds have excellent drainage so roots don't sit in standing water, which can lead to root rot.

### Fertilizer Advice for Next Month (Ammonium Sulfate)

You are correct to fertilize next month. **Ammonium sulfate ($\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$)** is an acceptable choice because it provides nitrogen in a form that supports the acidic nature of blueberry feeders, which is good.

*   **Why it works:** It supplies nitrogen (essential for strong vegetative growth) and sulfur, while maintaining acidity.
*   **Application Tips:**
    1.  **Follow Label Instructions Precisely:** Do not guess on dilution rates or application timings.
    2.  **Soil Test Confirmation:** If possible, supplement this with a soil test to confirm the exact nutrient deficiencies, but generally, ammonium sulfate is fine for boosting early-season growth.
    3.  **Complementary Nutrients (Long Term):** While ammonium sulfate focuses on Nitrogen ($\text{N}$), remember that once you start focusing heavily on *fruiting* (approaching Year 3), your fertilization needs will shift to include Phosphorus ($\text{P}$) and Potassium ($\text{K}$). Look for a balanced, low-Nitrogen fertilizer mix designed specifically for blueberries in the years leading up to fruiting time.

Here are the top 10 tips for starting a successful blueberry garden from scratch:

1.  **Know Your Soil Needs (pH is King):** Before planting anything, test your soil pH. Blueberries require acidic soil (ideally $\text{pH}$ 4.5–5.5). If your native soil is too alkaline, amending it heavily with sulfur-based materials or using acidifying fertilizers from the start is critical for success.
2.  **Choose the Right Variety:** Do not rely on just one variety. Planting three different varieties (like Emerald, Jewel, and Star) increases the chances that at least one will thrive based on your specific microclimate, as they may have varying levels of hardiness or bloom times.
3.  **Start with Proper Containers/Beds:** Blueberries hate "potting up" in poor soil. Use raised beds filled with a high-quality, acidic mix specifically designed for blueberries, rather than simply digging into existing lawn soil.
4.  **Mulch Heavily and Consistently:** Apply 2–3 inches of an appropriate mulch (like pine bark) around the base of the plants. This insulates roots, retains vital moisture, and helps stabilize the soil pH over time. *Keep the mulch away from directly touching the stems.*
5.  **Implement Targeted Watering Systems:** Drip irrigation is superior to overhead sprinklers because it delivers water slowly and directly to the root zone, minimizing water waste and keeping foliage dry (which reduces fungal diseases).
6.  **Scout for Pests Early and Often:** Never wait until an infestation is visible. Make regular "walk-throughs" of your garden, checking the undersides of leaves and monitoring new growth daily or every other day to catch minor issues (like aphids) before they become major outbreaks.
7.  **Mind Your Feeding Schedule:** Blueberries need careful feeding. Use slow-release, acidic fertilizers tailored for blueberries. When nitrogen levels drop off in later years, be prepared to supplement with phosphorus and potassium as the plants focus on fruit production.
8.  **Protect Against Wildlife (The Bird Factor):** As soon as berries begin to form, install protective netting over the entire area. Birds can consume a significant percentage of your nascent crop very quickly.
9.  **Practice Good Spacing:** When planting, follow the spacing guidelines recommended for mature bushes—this allows enough room for air circulation (preventing mildew) and ensures that the root systems don't compete too aggressively with each other for limited nutrients and water.
10. **Be Patient (It Takes Time):** Do not expect a massive yield in year one, or even year two. Blueberry plants are perennials that establish themselves slowly. Dedication to good soil management and consistent care over the first 3–4 years is what builds the foundation for a bumper crop in Year 3 and beyond.
