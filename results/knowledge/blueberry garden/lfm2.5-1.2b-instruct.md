Great questions! Here’s a breakdown tailored to your goals and current setup:

### **1. Pests & Diseases**
- **Common Blueberry Pests:** Besides aphids (you’ve already addressed with neem oil), keep an eye out for:
  - Spider mites (often in dry, hot conditions)
  - Whiteflies
  - Thrips
- **Diseases to Monitor:**
  - *Botrytis* (gray mold) – common in wet, humid environments; prune affected branches and ensure good airflow.
  - Root rot – avoid overwatering and ensure good drainage; use well-aerated raised beds.
  - Fusarium wilt – unlikely with acidic soil, but watch for yellowing or wilting.
- **Prevention Tips:**  
  - Remove fallen leaves/debris to reduce fungal spores.  
  - Avoid overhead watering; water at the base of plants.

### **2. Water Management**
- **Consistency is key:** Blueberries need reliable moisture—especially during fruiting and dormancy.  
  - For summer: Deep, infrequent watering (about 1–2 inches per week).  
  - For winter/dormant period: Ensure the mulch isn’t too thick; poke a few holes to let water drain.  
- **Tools:** Drip irrigation is ideal—it delivers water directly to roots and minimizes evaporation. If you’re using mulch, keep it 4–6 inches deep but not too wet.
- **Check Soil Moisture:** Stick your finger an inch into the soil—water when dry at that depth.

### **3. Fertilizer Recommendations**
Since you mentioned using ammonium sulfate next month, here’s how to go beyond:
- **Current Needs:** Blueberries thrive with a balanced fertilizer high in nitrogen (for foliage) and phosphorus/potassium (for fruit).  
  - A good choice: A 10-10-20 or 14-14-14 formula applied according to package directions.  
- **Application Timing:**  
  - Apply ammonium sulfate now (as planned), followed by a second application in midsummer if needed, and a third before fruiting (late spring).  
  - Avoid over-fertilizing; excess nitrogen can reduce berry quality.
- **Soil Test:** If you haven’t done one yet, a simple soil test will confirm pH levels and nutrient status—critical for acid-loving blueberries.

### **Extra Tips**
- **Pruning:** Trim back about 1/3 of the oldest growth each year to encourage new shoots.
- **Mulch Care:** Continue using pine bark mulch; refresh if it breaks down too quickly.
- **Harvest Timing:** You’re on track for a spring harvest—keep monitoring for ripeness (deep blue color and sweet aroma).

By staying vigilant with pests, watering consistently, and fine-tuning your fertilizer schedule, you’ll be well on your way to a bountiful blueberry crop! Let me know if you need help finding specific products or more details. 🍇

Here are the **top 10 tips** for starting a successful blueberry garden from scratch:

1. **Choose the Right Varieties:**  
   Pick acid-loving varieties suited to your region—like *Emerald*, *Jewel*, and *Star*—and check their mature size and blooming habits.

2. **Use Acidic Soil:**  
   Aim for pH 4.5–5.5. Mix in sulfur, peat moss, or elemental sulfur to lower the pH if needed; test soil regularly.

3. **Prepare Raised Beds:**  
   Build beds with a mix of compost, pine bark mulch, and coarse sand for drainage. This mimics their natural acidic environment.

4. **Plant at the Right Time:**  
   Plant in early spring or fall when temperatures are mild. Space bushes 2–3 feet apart to allow airflow.

5. **Water Consistently:**  
   Blueberries need deep, frequent watering (especially during fruiting). Aim for about 1 inch of water per week.

6. **Mulch Strategically:**  
   Apply pine bark mulch around bushes to retain moisture and suppress weeds—keep it 4–6 inches away from stems.

7. **Fertilize Regularly:**  
   Use a balanced fertilizer (like 10-10-20) or ammonium sulfate as recommended. Don’t overdo it!

8. **Prune for Health:**  
   Trim back old wood in late winter, and avoid cutting into new growth during fruiting season.

9. **Monitor Pests & Diseases Early:**  
   Watch for aphids, spider mites, or gray mold (Botrytis). Use neem oil or insecticidal soap as needed.

10. **Plan for Long-Term Success:**  
    Set aside time each season to inspect plants, adjust watering, and protect from wildlife with bird netting.

By following these steps, your blueberry garden will thrive—and you’ll enjoy fresh berries for jam, baking, and freezing! 🍇✨
