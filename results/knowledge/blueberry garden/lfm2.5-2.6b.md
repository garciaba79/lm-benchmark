**Application tip:** Apply at the rate recommended on the package (typically 1–2 lbs per season for established plants) but avoid over-fertilizing. ## Improving Your Blueberry Garden

### Pests to Watch For
Beyond the aphids you've already managed, keep an eye out for:

- **Spider mites** – Tiny webs on leaves and yellowing/drooping foliage. Increase watering during hot spells (mist daily) and use horticultural oil if needed.
- **Scale insects** – Small brown bumps on stems or new growth. Remove by prying off with a stiff brush, then apply insecticidal soap.
- **Berry mummies** – A fungal disease where mature berries turn dark, mushy, and develop a white fuzzy coating (caused by *Monilinia* spp.). Prune away infected canes immediately; this won't come back if you remove them.
- **Caterpillars & leafhoppers** – Can defoliate young plants. Hand-pick larger caterpillars or use row covers early in the season.

### Diseases to Monitor
- **Anthracnose** – Causes black, sunken spots on leaves and fruit. Prune out infected branches and destroy them (don't compost).
- **Root rot** – Common with poorly drained soil or excessive water. Look for wilting despite moist soil; improve drainage by adding sand or perlite to the raised bed mix.
- **Powdery mildew** – White, flour-like spots on leaves. Improve air circulation between plants and apply sulfur-based fungicide at first sign.

### Ensuring Adequate Water
Blueberries prefer **consistent moisture** but hate standing water:
- Your drip system is a good choice—check that emitters aren't clogged by flushing them occasionally.
- Mulch (pine bark) holds soil moisture, so maintain 2–3 inches of mulch depth. Replenish as it decomposes each year.
- During hot summer weeks (75°F+), water deeply every 5–7 days if rainfall is minimal. Blueberries need steady absorption—not just occasional flooding or drought stress.

### Fertilizer for Next Month
**Ammonium sulfate ((NH₄)₂SO₄)** is an excellent choice because it provides:
- **Nitrogen (N)** – Promotes strong new growth and fruit set
- **Sulfur (S)** – Many acidic soils are low in sulfur, which blueberries need

**Application details:**
- Measure the weight of your established plants (e.g., 6 bushes total). A standard rate is about **1–2 lbs per season** for each mature plant. For 6 bushes, that's roughly **8–12 lbs of ammonium sulfate**.
- Apply in late winter/early spring before new growth starts, or as you planned—next month fits well.
- **Important:** Water thoroughly after applying fertilizer to move nutrients into the root zone and prevent burn.

**Alternative option:** If you want a more balanced approach, consider using a **5-10-10 nitrogen fertilizer** (like a slow-release blueberry formula) alongside or instead of ammonium sulfate. This provides N without adding much sulfur, which can help if your soil is already naturally high in it.

**Avoid over-fertilizing:** More nitrogen = more leaves but fewer berries. If plants are looking lush with no fruit, back off on fertilizer next year. The goal at this stage (year 1) is strong growth and root development—berries will come naturally by year 2–3.

## Top 10 Tips for a Successful Blueberry Garden from Scratch

1. **Pick the Right Site**  
   Blueberries need **full sun (6–8 hours daily)** and **well-drained, acidic soil**. Test your pH—aim for 4.5–5.5. If your native soil is neutral or alkaline, amend it with elemental sulfur to lower the pH before planting.

2. **Prepare the Soil Thoroughly**  
   Work in plenty of organic matter (compost, leaf mold) and use a generous layer of **pine bark mulch** to maintain acidity and moisture. Blueberries are sensitive—poor soil is their biggest enemy.

3. **Source Healthy Plants Early**  
   Buy disease-free plants from a reputable nursery, preferably bare-root or container-grown from the same climate zone. Space bushes 4–5 feet apart in raised beds for airflow and easier management.

4. **Mulch Generously and Regularly**  
   A 2–3 inch layer of pine bark mulch conserves moisture, suppresses weeds, and keeps soil pH stable. Replenish annually as it decomposes.

5. **Install a Reliable Irrigation System**  
   Drip irrigation is ideal for blueberries—it delivers water directly to the root zone without wetting foliage (which reduces disease risk). Check emitters monthly; clogged lines can cause uneven moisture and fruit cracking.

6. **Fertilize with Ammonium Sulfate (or a Blueberry-Specific Formula)**  
   Apply **1–2 lbs per season** of ammonium sulfate or use a balanced slow-release fertilizer at the start of spring before new growth emerges. This provides nitrogen for growth and sulfur, which many blueberry varieties need.

7. **Prune Strategically After First Harvest**  
   In your second year (after fruiting begins), thin out old, weak canes and remove crossed or inward-growing branches to open the center of the bush. This improves air circulation and light penetration—both critical for big berry yields.

8. **Monitor for Pests Proactively**  
   Watch for aphids (spray neem oil early), spider mites (increase watering, apply horticultural oil if needed), scale insects, and berry mummies. Early detection prevents infestations from spreading.

9. **Scout for Diseases Regularly**  
   Keep an eye out for anthracnose (black spots on leaves/fruit) and root rot (wilting despite moist soil). Remove infected canes immediately and avoid composting diseased material—this protects the rest of your crop.

10. **Harvest at Peak Ripeness and Preserve Carefully**  
    Blueberries are ready when fully colored but still firm. Pick in the morning after dew has dried for maximum sweetness. For jams and freezing, use a quick 1–2 minute water bath canning method or flash-freeze on a tray before bagging.

---

**Bonus Tip:** Rotate your blueberry bushes every 4–5 years if you plan to plant another crop in the same spot. This reduces soil-borne disease buildup and maintains long-term productivity.

Good luck with your new garden—you're off to a great start!
