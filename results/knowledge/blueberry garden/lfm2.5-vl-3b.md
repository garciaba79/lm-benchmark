## 1. Ammonium Sulfate – timing & rate

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| **When to apply** | Early spring, just before the new growth begins (usually March–April in most temperate zones). |
| **Recommended rate** | **2 lb per bush** (≈ 0.9 kg) of AS dissolved in water. For 6 bushes: **12 lb total**. |
| **How to mix** | • Dissolve the AS in a bucket of water (use distilled or filtered water if possible).<br>• Add a small amount of mild liquid fertilizer (e.g., 1 tbsp per gallon) to help the plants absorb the nitrogen.<br>• Apply evenly around the base of each bush, keeping it away from the trunk. |
| **Safety tip** | Wear gloves and eye protection; AS is irritating to skin and eyes. If you’re using a sprayer, keep it off foliage that will be eaten or frozen. |

## 2. Soil & Mulch – what to watch for

- **pH check:** Test the soil again after fertilizing (or at least every 1–2 years). Blueberries need pH 4.5–5.5; if it drifts above 5.5, consider a light application of elemental sulfur or a slow‑release acid fertilizer.
- **Mulch depth:** Keep the mulch 2–3 inches thick around the drip emitters but **not touching** the stems to prevent rot and disease.
- **Watering:** With AS, the soil may become slightly more acidic. Continue regular watering (drip system) to keep the root zone moist; avoid over‑watering which can leach nutrients.

## 3. Bird protection – netting basics

| Step | What to do |
|------|-------------|
| **Choose a mesh** | Use a **fine‑mesh bird netting** (≥ ½‑inch or 12 mm holes) that’s rated for at least 30 lb of weight per square foot. |
| **Install the frame** | Build a simple rectangular cage around each bush using garden stakes, bamboo poles, or reclaimed wood. Secure it with zip ties or galvanized wire. |
| **Attach to the drip system** | Run a short piece of tubing from the main line to the base of each plant and tie the netting to that tube so the birds can’t climb up. |
| **Check for gaps** | Inspect after planting; any holes larger than ½ in will let birds in. Reinforce with additional mesh or a lightweight screen if needed. |

---

## 4. Fertilizer & feeding schedule (beyond AS)

- **Organic amendment:** After the first harvest, add a thin layer of well‑rotted compost or leaf mold around each bush to replenish organic matter and improve soil structure.
- **Second dose (mid‑summer):** A light application of **balanced 10‑10‑10** fertilizer (or a slow‑release nitrogen‑phosphorus‑potassium blend) can help the plants store sugars for winter hardiness and next spring’s fruit set.
- **Avoid over‑fertilizing:** Too much nitrogen can promote vegetative growth at the expense of berries, and excess potassium may cause blossom end rot.

## 2. Pests & Diseases – What to Watch For

### Common Blueberry Issues
| Pest / Disease | Typical Signs | Prevention / Control |
|----------------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Aphids** (already seen) | Small, soft‑bodied insects on new growth; honeydew and sooty mold. | • Spray with neem oil or insecticidal soap every 7–10 days during active growth.<br>• Encourage lady beetles, lacewings, and parasitic wasps by planting a few companion flowers (e.g., dill, yarrow). |
| **Spider Mites** | Fine webbing on leaves; yellowing or stippling. | • Increase humidity with a misting system or a pebble tray.<br>• Apply neem oil or insecticidal soap; treat early before mites reproduce. |
| **Blueberry Wilt (Phytophthora)** | Yellowing, wilted foliage, especially in the center of the plant; roots may be dark and mushy when dug.<br>**Note:** This is a soil‑borne disease that can spread quickly. | • Ensure excellent drainage; avoid overwatering.<br>• Use a raised bed with good aeration (pitchfork or sand).<br>• Remove any infected plants promptly and destroy them (do not compost). |

| **Fungal Spots (e.g., Powdery Mildew)** | White powdery coating on leaves, especially in humid conditions. | • Improve air circulation; prune to open canopy.<br>• Apply a copper‑based fungicide (e.g., Bordeaux) at first sign of mildew. |

### Early‑Detection Checklist
- **Weekly visual scan:** Look for new growth, discoloration, webbing, or tiny insects on the undersides of leaves.
- **Soil moisture check:** Stick a finger 2–3 inches into the soil; if it feels dry, water now (preferably in the morning to reduce evaporation).
- **Leaf inspection:** Look for yellowing edges, stippling, or honeydew.

## 3. Watering Throughout the Season

| Phase | Recommended Practice |
|-------|-----------------------|
| **Planting & Establishment** | • Water deeply once after planting (about 1–2 inches).<br>• Keep soil consistently moist for the first 6–8 weeks, then reduce to every 7–10 days as plants mature. |
| **Growth (Spring‑Summer)** | • Aim for **1–1.5 inches of water per week** total (including rain).<br>• Use a drip system with a timer set to run early morning or late evening to minimize evaporation.<br>• Mulch helps retain moisture; check that the top 2–3 inches stay damp but not soggy. |
| **Fruit Set & Ripening** | • Increase frequency slightly (every 5–7 days) as berries develop, especially during hot spells.<br>• Avoid over‑watering at this stage to prevent root rot and fungal issues. |
| **Winter Prep** | • Reduce watering to once a week in late fall/early winter; the plants go dormant and need less moisture. |

### Practical Tips
- **Soil moisture meter:** A simple probe (or a handful of soil) can tell you if you’re over‑ or under‑watering.
- **Mulch depth:** Maintain 2–3 inches to keep roots cool and retain water.
- **Avoid wetting foliage:** Water at the base, not from above, to reduce leaf disease.

**Top 10 Tips for Starting a Successful Blueberry Garden (from Scratch)**  

1. **Pick the Right Varieties & Site**  
   - Choose hardy, disease‑resistant cultivars suited to your climate zone (e.g., ‘Duke’, ‘Madeira’, or ‘Patriot’).  
   - Plant in full sun (6–8 hrs/day) with well‑draining soil; avoid low‑lying areas that stay wet.

2. **Test & Amend Soil pH**  
   - Blueberries thrive at pH 4.5–5.5. Use a soil test kit or send samples to a lab.  
   - If pH is too high, apply elemental sulfur (1–2 lb per 100 sq ft) in early fall; if too low, add lime gradually.

3. **Prepare Raised Beds**  
   - Build beds 12–18 inches wide and 4–6 feet deep with a mix of peat moss, pine bark, and compost (about 60‑70 % organic matter).  
   - Ensure good drainage; add coarse sand or perlite if the native soil is heavy.

4. **Install Proper Irrigation**  
   - Set up drip irrigation or soaker hoses to deliver water directly to the root zone, reducing evaporation and leaf wetness (which promotes fungal disease).

5. **Use Acidic Mulch**  
   - Apply 2–3 inches of pine bark mulch around each plant; keep it a few inches away from stems to prevent rot.

6. **Plant at Correct Spacing & Depth**  
   - Space bushes 4–6 feet apart (or follow the label spacing). Plant them at the same depth they were in their containers, firming soil gently.

7. **Protect Against Birds Early On**  
   - Install fine‑mesh bird netting or a cage around each bush as soon as plants are established to deter fruit theft.

8. **Start with Organic Feeding**  
   - After planting, work in a thin layer of well‑rotted compost and apply a balanced 10‑10‑10 fertilizer (or sulfur if pH is high) at the recommended rate.

9. **Water Consistently & Mulch Year‑Round**  
   - Aim for ~1 inch of water per week; adjust during hot spells or dry periods. Keep mulch in place to retain moisture and suppress weeds.

10. **Monitor & Maintain Ongoingly**  
    - Inspect foliage weekly for aphids, spider mites, or early signs of disease (e.g., powdery mildew). Treat promptly with neem oil or insecticidal soap. Prune dead or crossing canes in late winter to improve air flow and fruit quality.

Following these ten steps will give you a solid foundation for a productive, healthy blueberry garden from the very first planting season. Happy growing!
