Here’s tailored advice for your blueberry garden:

**Pests & Diseases to Watch For:**  
- **Common Pests:** Beyond aphids (already managed), watch for spider mites, blueberry gall midge, and Japanese beetles. Use sticky traps or insecticidal soap if needed.  
- **Diseases:** Root rot (from overwatering), powdery mildew, and leaf spot are risks in humid climates. Ensure good air circulation, avoid overhead watering, and remove infected leaves.  
- **Prevention:** Rotate crops annually if possible, and sanitize tools to prevent spreading pathogens.

**Water Management:**  
- Keep soil consistently moist but not soggy—blueberries prefer evenly damp conditions.  
- Drip irrigation is ideal; consider adding a moisture sensor or checking soil 2–3 inches deep weekly.  
- Mulch helps retain moisture, but avoid piling mulch against stems to prevent rot.

**Fertilizer Recommendation:**  
- For next month’s application, use **ammonium sulfate** (as you planned) at about 1 tablespoon per bush, worked into the soil around the drip line. Avoid over-application, which can harm roots.

**Additional Tips:**  
- Prune in late winter to remove old, unproductive stems and encourage new growth.  
- Test soil pH annually; if it drifts above 5.5, amend with sulfur to maintain acidity.  
- Thin fruit in early summer if clusters are very dense—this improves berry size and plant health.

Here are the top 10 actionable tips for launching a thriving blueberry garden:

1. **Prioritize Acidic Soil (pH 4.5–5.5):** Test soil first; amend with sulfur or peat moss if needed to maintain acidity—blueberries won’t thrive otherwise.  
2. **Choose Sunny, Well-Drained Sites:** Aim for 6+ hours of direct sunlight daily and avoid low-lying areas prone to frost pockets or waterlogging.  
3. **Plant at the Right Time:** Spring (after frost) or early fall (in mild climates) ensures roots establish before extreme heat/cold. Space bushes 4–5 feet apart.  
4. **Use Raised Beds with Organic Matter:** Mix in compost, pine bark, and peat moss to boost fertility and drainage—critical for root health.  
5. **Mulch Generously (2–4 inches):** Pine bark or wood chips retain moisture, suppress weeds, and acidify soil as they decompose. Keep mulch 3 inches from stems.  
6. **Install Drip Irrigation:** Provides consistent moisture without overwatering—blueberries hate soggy roots. Adjust for rainfall.  
7. **Fertilize Strategically:** Use ammonium sulfate (1 tbsp/bush in spring) or organic blends (e.g., blood meal + kelp). Avoid high-phosphorus fertilizers.  
8. **Prune Annually (Late Winter):** Remove 1/3 of old stems to boost new growth and airflow—prevents disease and improves yields.  
9. **Protect from Birds & Pests:** Install netting *before* fruit sets; use row covers for aphids/mites. Inspect weekly for early issues.  
10. **Plan for Long-Term Care:** Thin young fruit to 6–8 inches apart, monitor pH yearly, and rotate crops every 5–7 years if possible.  

**Bonus Tip:** Start with 3–4 varieties (like your Emerald/Jewel/Star mix) for staggered harvests and better pollination!
