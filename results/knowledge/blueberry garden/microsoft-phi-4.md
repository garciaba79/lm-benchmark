Improving your blueberry garden and ensuring healthy, productive plants involves a combination of good practices for pest management, disease prevention, adequate watering, and proper fertilization. Here’s some advice on these aspects:

### Improvements and Maintenance

1. **Soil Health**: 
   - Continue to monitor soil pH. Blueberries prefer acidic conditions (pH 4.5–5.5). You might need to test your soil annually.
   - Amend the soil with organic matter, like pine bark or peat moss, if needed, to maintain acidity and improve soil structure.

2. **Pruning**: 
   - Regularly prune your blueberry bushes in late winter or early spring before new growth starts. Remove any dead wood, thin out crowded branches, and shape the bush for optimal light penetration and air circulation.
   
3. **Mulching**:
   - Keep mulch at least 2-4 inches deep around plants to conserve moisture, suppress weeds, and maintain soil temperature.

### Pests and Diseases

1. **Common Pests**:
   - **Aphids**: You've already dealt with aphids using neem oil; continue monitoring for these pests.
   - **Blueberry Maggot**: Look out for maggots inside berries, which can be controlled by burying infested berries and using traps or insecticides if necessary.
   - **Spotted Wing Drosophila**: This fruit fly lays eggs in ripening fruit. Use exclusion nets when the fruit starts to ripen.

2. **Diseases**:
   - **Fruit Rot (e.g., Mucor, Botrytis)**: Ensure good air circulation around plants and remove affected berries promptly.
   - **Leaf Spot**: Remove and destroy fallen leaves and consider a fungicide if it becomes severe.
   - **Root Rot**: Maintain proper drainage to prevent waterlogged soil conditions.

### Watering

1. **Consistent Moisture**:
   - Blueberries require consistent moisture, especially during fruit development. Your drip irrigation setup is ideal for this purpose.
   - Aim for 1-2 inches of water per week, depending on rainfall and temperature. Adjust your irrigation system accordingly.

2. **Check Soil Moisture**:
   - Use a soil moisture meter or check the top few inches of soil manually to ensure it stays moist but not soggy.

### Fertilization

1. **Ammonium Sulfate**: You mentioned using ammonium sulfate, which is excellent for providing nitrogen without raising soil pH.
   
2. **Balanced Fertilizer**:
   - In addition to nitrogen, consider a balanced fertilizer that includes phosphorus and potassium, especially in the first few years of growth.
   - Look for fertilizers specifically formulated for acid-loving plants or those with an N-P-K ratio of 10-5-5 or similar.

3. **Application Timing**:
   - Apply fertilizer when new growth begins in spring but avoid late-season applications that can promote tender growth susceptible to winter damage.

By focusing on these aspects, you'll help ensure your blueberry bushes remain healthy and productive. If any specific issues arise, feel free to seek further advice tailored to those situations!

Starting a successful blueberry garden involves careful planning and consistent care. Here are the top 10 tips to guide you through the process:

1. **Choose the Right Variety**:
   - Select varieties that are well-suited to your climate zone. Some popular options include 'Highbush' for colder climates, while 'Rabbiteye' is better for warmer areas.

2. **Location and Sunlight**:
   - Plant your blueberries in a location where they receive at least 6-8 hours of full sun daily. Ensure good air circulation to reduce disease risk.

3. **Soil Requirements**:
   - Blueberries thrive in acidic soil with a pH between 4.5 and 5.5. Use soil tests to determine your garden's pH and amend it with sulfur or peat moss if necessary.

4. **Planting Space**:
   - Allow adequate space for air circulation and growth, typically planting bushes about 3-5 feet apart within rows that are spaced 6-8 feet apart.

5. **Water Management**:
   - Ensure consistent moisture without waterlogging the roots. Use drip irrigation or soaker hoses to provide even watering directly to the root zone.

6. **Mulching**:
   - Apply a 2-4 inch layer of organic mulch, such as pine bark or wood chips, around plants to conserve moisture, suppress weeds, and maintain soil acidity.

7. **Fertilization**:
   - Start with an acidic fertilizer like ammonium sulfate or one specifically formulated for acid-loving plants. Follow up with additional applications during the growing season as needed.

8. **Pruning**:
   - Prune your blueberry bushes in late winter to early spring before new growth begins. Remove dead wood and thin out crowded branches to improve air circulation and sunlight penetration.

9. **Pest and Disease Management**:
   - Monitor plants regularly for pests like aphids, blueberry maggot, or spotted wing drosophila. Use organic controls such as neem oil or insect traps when needed.
   - Prevent diseases by removing fallen leaves and pruning to ensure good air flow around the bushes.

10. **Bird Protection**:
    - Protect ripening fruit from birds with netting or row covers once berries start to form, especially if bird populations are high in your area.

By following these tips, you'll set a strong foundation for a thriving blueberry garden that will yield delicious fruits year after year.
