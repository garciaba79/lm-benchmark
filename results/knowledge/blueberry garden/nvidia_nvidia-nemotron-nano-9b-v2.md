   - Pick varieties suited to your climate (e.g., Emerald, Jewel, Star) and consider early, mid, or late-season types for staggered harvests.

3. **Prepare the Planting Site**:  
   - Dig raised beds or amend native soil with organic matter (pine bark, compost) to improve drainage and acidity. Avoid compacted soil.

4. **Plant Correctly**:  
   - Space bushes 4–6 feet apart for airflow. Plant at the same depth as the nursery container, ensuring roots aren’t buried too deep.

5. **Establish a Watering Routine**:  
   - Use drip irrigation or soaker hoses to maintain consistent moisture (1–2 inches weekly). Mulch heavily with pine bark to retain water.

6. **Fertilize Strategically**:  
   - Start with ammonium sulfate in early spring (1/4 cup per plant) for nitrogen. Later, use a balanced fertilizer (e.g., 10-10-10) as berries develop.

7. **Manage Pests & Diseases**:  
   - Monitor for aphids, blueberry maggot, and spider mites. Use neem oil or insecticidal soap early. Prevent powdery mildew with good airflow and avoid wetting foliage.

8. **Prune Annually**:  
   - Prune in late winter to remove dead wood, thin crowded branches, and encourage fruiting canes. Remove 1/3 of old wood each year.

9. **Protect from Birds & Wildlife**:  
   - Install netting or use reflective tape early in the season. Harvest berries promptly once ripe to deter theft.

10. **Plan for Long-Term Care**:  
    - Test soil pH yearly, renew mulch, and adjust care as plants mature. Blueberries can produce for 15+ years with proper maintenance.
