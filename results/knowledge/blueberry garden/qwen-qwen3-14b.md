Great questions! Here's tailored advice based on your current setup:

### 1. **Improving Your Blueberry Garden:**
- **Soil Testing:** Even though you've set up an acidic soil mix, consider testing the soil annually to ensure pH stays in the ideal range (4.5–5.5). Adjust with sulfur if it rises or lime if it drops.
- **Spacing and Pruning:** Ensure plants have enough space—about 3–4 feet apart—to allow for air circulation and sunlight penetration. Prune annually during dormancy to remove dead or weak branches, which helps improve yield and disease resistance.
- **Mulching:** Continue using pine bark mulch (or pine needles), as it helps maintain soil acidity, moisture, and suppresses weeds.

### 2. **Pests and Diseases to Watch For:**
- **Common Pests:**
  - **Aphids** – You've already dealt with a few, so regular monitoring is key. Neem oil or insecticidal soap can be effective.
  - **Japanese beetles, plum curculio (if in your area), and fruitworms** – Use row covers early in the season if needed, or apply organic sprays like spinosad as a preventive measure.
- **Common Diseases:**
  - **Mummy berry** – Look for shriveled, mummified berries on the plant. Remove infected fruit promptly and consider applying a fungicide (like captan) during bloom.
  - **Powdery mildew** – Watch for white, powdery patches on leaves. Good air circulation helps prevent it; use organic sprays like sulfur if needed.

### 3. **Watering Throughout the Season:**
- Blueberries require consistent moisture—ideally about 1–2 inches of water per week.
- Use your drip irrigation system efficiently:
  - Water deeply but infrequently to encourage deep root growth.
  - Avoid overwatering, which can lead to root rot.
  - Check soil moisture regularly; the top inch should be slightly dry before watering again.

### 4. **Fertilizer for Next Month:**
- You’re on track by planning to use **ammonium sulfate**, which is ideal for blueberries because it provides nitrogen in a form that’s easily absorbed and won’t raise soil pH.
- Apply about **1–2 pounds of ammonium sulfate per 100 square feet** around the drip line, not directly against the trunk.
- After applying fertilizer, water thoroughly to help nutrients reach the roots.

By following these tips, you'll be well on your way to a healthy, productive blueberry garden. Keep monitoring and stay consistent with care routines!

Provide top 10 tips for starting a successful blueberry garden from scratch.

Here are the **Top 10 Tips for Starting a Successful Blueberry Garden from Scratch**:

1. **Choose the Right Varieties:** Select disease-resistant, cold-hardy varieties suited to your climate (e.g., Northern Highbush for colder regions or Southern Highbush for warmer climates). Consider cross-pollination by planting at least two different varieties.

2. **Test and Prepare Soil Properly:** Blueberries thrive in acidic soil with a pH of 4.5–5.5. Test your soil annually and amend it with peat moss, sulfur, or compost to achieve the right pH.

3. **Use Raised Beds or Mounded Rows:** Blueberry roots are shallow and sensitive to poor drainage. Raised beds or mounded rows help maintain proper moisture levels and soil conditions.

4. **Install a Drip Irrigation System:** Consistent watering is critical for blueberries. A drip irrigation system delivers water directly to the root zone, reducing waste and preventing overwatering.

5. **Mulch with Acidic Materials:** Apply pine bark, pine needles, or wood chips as mulch to help retain moisture, suppress weeds, and maintain soil acidity.

6. **Fertilize Correctly:** Use fertilizers high in nitrogen (like ammonium sulfate) early in the season and switch to balanced, acid-based fertilizers later. Avoid over-fertilizing with nitrogen after mid-summer.

7. **Provide Proper Spacing:** Plant blueberry bushes 3–4 feet apart within rows, with rows at least 8–10 feet apart. This allows for good air circulation and reduces disease risk.

8. **Monitor for Pests and Diseases:** Watch for aphids, Japanese beetles, and mummy berry disease. Use organic controls like neem oil, insecticidal soap, or row covers to protect plants early in the season.

9. **Prune Regularly:** Prune during dormancy (late winter) to remove dead or weak branches, improve air circulation, and encourage new growth that produces fruit.

10. **Protect from Birds and Other Pests:** Once berries begin forming, install bird netting or use scare tactics to keep birds away. Use physical barriers or deterrents for other animals if needed.

By following these tips, you'll set yourself up for a thriving blueberry garden that provides fresh, delicious fruit year after year!
