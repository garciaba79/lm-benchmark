Here's my advice for improving your blueberry garden:

**Pests & Diseases to Watch For:**
- **Blueberry maggots** - small flies that lay eggs in developing berries
- **Spider mites** - look for stippling on leaves, especially in dry conditions
- **Phomopsis blight** - causes canker spots on stems and dieback
- **Root rots** - watch for wilting, yellowing leaves, especially after heavy rains
- **Scale insects** - small, immobile pests that appear as brown spots on stems

**Water Management:**
- Blueberries need 1-2 inches of water weekly (including rainfall)
- Drip irrigation is excellent - keep it running 15-20 minutes daily during growing season
- Check soil moisture by sticking your finger 2 inches down - it should feel moist but not soggy
- Mulch helps retain moisture and regulate soil temperature

**Fertilizer for Next Month:**
- **Ammonium sulfate** (21-0-0) is perfect - apply 1/4 cup per plant
- Or use a specialized acid-loving plant fertilizer like 10-10-10 or 12-12-12
- Apply in early spring before new growth starts
- Spread evenly around the drip line, not directly on stems

**Additional Tips:**
- Add more pine bark mulch as needed throughout growing season
- Consider planting a few companion herbs like thyme or rosemary to deter some pests
- Monitor for birds starting late summer - they'll be after the ripening berries

Here are the top 10 tips for starting a successful blueberry garden:

1. **Test and adjust soil pH** - Blueberries need acidic soil (4.5-5.5). Test your soil in fall or early spring, and amend with sulfur or peat moss if needed.

2. **Choose the right location** - Select a spot with full sun (6-8 hours daily), good drainage, and protection from harsh winds.

3. **Use raised beds or mounding** - Blueberries don't tolerate wet feet. Create raised beds or mound soil to ensure proper drainage.

4. **Plant multiple varieties** - Choose 2-3 different varieties for better pollination and extended harvest season (early, mid, late season).

5. **Prepare soil properly** - Mix in plenty of organic matter like peat moss, compost, and pine bark mulch before planting.

6. **Install drip irrigation early** - Blueberries need consistent moisture but don't like wet feet. Drip systems deliver water directly to roots.

7. **Apply mulch immediately** - Use pine bark, wood chips, or pine needles (2-3 inches thick) to retain moisture and maintain acidity.

8. **Fertilize appropriately** - Use acid-loving fertilizers (like ammonium sulfate or 10-10-10) in early spring, avoiding lime or alkaline fertilizers.

9. **Protect from birds** - Install netting or use bird scare devices as berries begin to ripen to prevent crop loss.

10. **Be patient and persistent** - Blueberries take 2-3 years to establish and produce heavily. Don't expect big harvests the first year.
