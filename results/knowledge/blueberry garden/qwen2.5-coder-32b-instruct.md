### Pest and Disease Management

1. **Pests**:
   - **Aphids**: You've already dealt with these using neem oil, which is good. Keep checking plants regularly.
   - **Spotted Wing Drosophila (SWD)**: This fruit fly can be a significant problem for blueberries. Use yellow sticky traps to monitor their presence and remove any infested berries immediately.
   - **Japanese Beetles**: These can chew leaves and damage flowers. Hand-picking them is effective, or you can use row covers early in the season to prevent them from getting to your plants.
   - **Scale Insects**: Look for small brown bumps on stems; these can be treated with horticultural oil.

2. **Diseases**:
   - **Mummy Berry Disease**: This fungal disease affects berries, making them shriveled and covered in a gray coating. Remove infected berries and fallen leaves promptly.
   - **Powdery Mildew**: A white powdery growth on leaves can reduce plant vigor. Improve air circulation around plants and avoid overhead watering.
   - **Rust Fungi**: Orange spots on the undersides of leaves indicate rust. Prune out affected parts and use fungicides if necessary.

### Watering

1. **Drip Irrigation**: Your setup is ideal for maintaining consistent moisture levels without wetting foliage, which can help prevent fungal diseases.
2. **Soil Moisture**: Blueberries need consistent moisture, especially from early spring through harvest. Aim to keep the soil evenly moist but not waterlogged. Check soil moisture regularly, and adjust watering as needed based on rainfall and temperature.

### Fertilization

1. **Type of Fertilizer**: Ammonium sulfate is a good choice for blueberries because it increases soil acidity and provides nitrogen. It's especially useful in the first few years to promote growth.
2. **Application Rate**: The general recommendation is to apply about 1/2 pound of ammonium sulfate per plant annually, divided into three applications (early spring, mid-spring, and early summer). Adjust based on your specific soil test results if available.

Certainly! Here are the top 10 tips for starting a successful blueberry garden from scratch:

### 1. **Choose the Right Variety**
- Select varieties that are well-suited to your climate and soil conditions. Consider early, mid-season, and late season berries to extend your harvest period.
- Popular varieties include: 'Emerald', 'Jewel', 'Star', 'Bluecrop', 'Duke', and 'Chandler'.

### 2. **Test Your Soil**
- Test your soil to determine its pH and nutrient levels. Blueberries require acidic soil with a pH range of 4.5 to 5.5.
- If the soil is too alkaline, you can lower the pH by adding sulfur or peat moss.

### 3. **Prepare the Site**
- Choose a sunny location where plants will receive at least 6-8 hours of direct sunlight daily.
- Ensure good drainage; blueberries do not like standing water. If your site has poor drainage, consider raised beds or mounds.

### 4. **Amend the Soil**
- Incorporate organic matter such as compost, peat moss, and pine bark into the soil to improve structure and nutrient content.
- Mix in sulfur if necessary to adjust the pH to the optimal range for blueberries.

### 5. **Planting**
- Plant your blueberry bushes in early spring or fall when they are dormant.
- Space bushes about 4-6 feet apart to allow for air circulation and growth.
- Dig a hole slightly larger than the root ball, and plant so that the top of the roots are just above soil level.

### 6. **Watering**
- Water regularly, especially in dry periods, to keep the soil consistently moist but not waterlogged.
- Use drip irrigation or soaker hoses to deliver water directly to the roots, reducing leaf wetness and minimizing disease risk.

### 7. **Mulching**
- Apply a 2-3 inch layer of organic mulch (e.g., pine bark, wood chips) around the base of each plant to retain moisture, suppress weeds, and regulate soil temperature.
- Replenish the mulch annually as it decomposes.

### 8. **Fertilizing**
- Use an acidic fertilizer such as ammonium sulfate or a balanced blend designed for acid-loving plants.
- Apply in early spring and again in mid-summer, following package instructions for amounts based on plant size and age.

### 9. **Pruning**
- Remove any dead, diseased, or weak branches in late winter or early spring to encourage new growth and improve air circulation.
- Thin out older branches every few years to maintain a healthy shape and fruit production.

### 10. **Pest and Disease Management**
- Regularly monitor your plants for signs of pests and diseases.
- Use organic methods like neem oil, insecticidal soap, or horticultural oil for pest control.
- Remove fallen leaves and fruit debris regularly to prevent the spread of fungal diseases.
- Consider netting during harvest time to protect berries from birds.

By following these 10 tips, you'll be well on your way to establishing a healthy and productive blueberry garden. Enjoy your gardening journey!
