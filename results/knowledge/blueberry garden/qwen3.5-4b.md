Here is specific advice to improve your blueberry garden based on your current setup:

**Improvement Strategies**
*   **Pruning:** Prune lightly in late winter or early spring to remove dead wood, thin out crowded branches for better air circulation, and shape the canopy. This improves light penetration and fruit quality.
*   **Pollination:** Verify if your varieties (Emerald, Jewel, Star) are self-fertile. If they require cross-pollination, ensure bees have access to open flowers during bloom. If yields are low, consider adding a compatible pollinator variety.
*   **Spacing:** Ensure bushes are spaced at least 3–4 feet apart in raised beds to allow adequate airflow and root space.

**Pests and Diseases to Monitor**
*   **Spider Mites:** Look for fine webbing on leaves or stippling (tiny yellow dots) under the leaf surface. They thrive in dry conditions. Treat with neem oil or insecticidal soap if infestation is detected.
*   **Powdery Mildew:** Appears as white, flour-like spots on leaves and stems. Use a copper-based fungicide spray during humid periods to prevent spread.
*   **Fruit Borers:** Inspect berries for holes or frass (sawdust-like droppings) after harvest. Remove infested fruit immediately to stop larvae from spreading.
*   **Root Rot:** Since you are using raised beds, ensure drainage is excellent. If leaves turn yellow and wilt despite watering, check for root rot caused by overwatering or poor soil structure.

**Water Management**
*   **Drip Irrigation Maintenance:** Check your drip lines monthly to prevent clogging from pine bark mulch debris. Ensure emitters are not leaking excessively into the bed center.
*   **Consistency:** Blueberries prefer consistent moisture but do not tolerate "wet feet." Water deeply when soil is dry, rather than frequent light sprinkling.
*   **Mulch Depth:** Maintain a 2–3 inch layer of pine bark mulch around the base to retain moisture and suppress weeds without touching the stems directly (to prevent rot).

**Fertilizer for Next Month**
*   **Type:** Continue using **ammonium sulfate**. It provides nitrogen while maintaining soil acidity, which is critical for blueberries.
*   **Supplement:** If you notice yellowing leaves (chlorosis), supplement with an iron chelate fertilizer to prevent nutrient deficiency without altering pH too much.
*   **Application:** Apply before bud break in early spring to support vigorous growth and fruit set. Follow package rates strictly to avoid salt buildup, which can damage roots.

> #### 🤖 Assistant

1.  **Test Soil pH First:** Ensure soil acidity is between 4.5 and 5.5; blueberries will not thrive in neutral or alkaline conditions, so amend with sulfur or peat moss if necessary before planting.
2.  **Select Compatible Varieties:** Choose varieties that cross-pollinate well together (e.g., Emerald, Jewel, Star) to maximize fruit set, as most blueberry bushes require a pollinator partner for optimal yield.
3.  **Provide Full Sunlight:** Plant bushes in locations receiving at least six to eight hours of direct sunlight daily to ensure strong growth and abundant flowering.
4.  **Use Raised Beds or Amended Soil:** Raised beds offer better drainage control, but if planting in ground, mix in peat moss or pine bark to maintain the required acidic pH structure.
5.  **Apply Acidic Mulch:** Use pine bark mulch to retain moisture and suppress weeds, but keep it at least two inches away from the plant stems to prevent rot.
6.  **Install Drip Irrigation:** Set up a drip system to deliver consistent water directly to the root zone, avoiding overwatering or dry spells that stress the plants.
7.  **Fertilize with Acidic Nutrients:** Use fertilizers like ammonium sulfate during the growing season to provide nitrogen without raising soil pH too high.
8.  **Prune Annually in Late Winter:** Remove dead wood and thin crowded branches before spring growth begins to improve air circulation and light penetration into the canopy.
9.  **Monitor for Pests and Diseases:** Regularly inspect leaves for spider mites, powdery mildew, or root rot, and treat with organic options like neem oil or copper fungicides early in infestation.
10. **Protect Fruit from Birds:** Install bird netting over the bushes during bloom and harvest seasons to prevent fruit loss while ensuring bees can still access flowers for pollination.
