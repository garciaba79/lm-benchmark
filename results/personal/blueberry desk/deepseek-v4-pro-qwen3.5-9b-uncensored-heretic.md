### 1. Milling Strategy: Working with the Irregularities

Your custom rig on the bandsaw is a great idea for ripping round wood into straighter pieces. However, given the small thickness (1-3") and potential bends, consider these tactics:

*   **Embrace the Bend:** Instead of trying to force every piece perfectly flat, use the natural curve as part of your design. A slightly curved top can be very organic and interesting for a minimalist desk.
*   **Glue Up Early:** Since you have small pieces, glue up multiple thin slabs (e.g., 1" or 2") into thicker boards *before* flattening them on the bandsaw. This creates larger, more stable panels that are easier to work with later.
*   **Focus on Stability, Not Perfection:** A desk top doesn't need to be perfectly flat down to a hair's breadth. Small warps or slight curves can actually add character and reduce stress points during drying.

### 2. Desk Design: Minimalist & Efficient

Your minimalist design with simple legs and apron is perfect for this situation. Here are some specific suggestions:

*   **Legs:** Use turned legs (lathe work) from the thicker ends of your trunks, or simply cut them straight from the thickest parts. A simple square leg or a slightly tapered leg works well.
*   **Apron:** The apron is crucial for structural integrity and aesthetics. Consider making it out of a single wide piece glued up from multiple slabs to create a continuous grain pattern across the front. This also hides any minor gaps between pieces.
*   **Top Thickness:** Aim for 1" or 1.5" thickness for the top. This is thick enough to be sturdy but thin enough to minimize waste and make it easier to flatten slightly bent pieces.

### 3. Grain Orientation: Choosing Your Aesthetic

This is where you can really make your limited wood supply shine. Here are the three options, ranked by suitability for your situation:

*   **Face-Grain (Most Recommended):** This shows the most dramatic grain pattern and color variation. It's the classic choice for a desk top because it looks like a slab of wood cut from a tree. You can glue up multiple slabs to create a wide panel with interesting patterns.
 * *Pros:* Maximum visual impact, hides minor surface imperfections well.
 * *Cons:* Requires careful flattening to avoid cupping or bowing during drying.

*   **Edge-Grain:** This shows the growth rings as parallel lines running along the length of the board. It's more subtle and can look very elegant if done right.
 * *Pros:* Less prone to warping than face-grain, easier to flatten slightly bent pieces.
 * *Cons:* Can look a bit "boring" if not carefully selected; requires precise cutting to get clean lines.

*   **End-Grain:** This shows the cross-section of the tree rings as concentric circles or arcs. It's very striking but can be tricky to work with because it's often more prone to cracking during drying and is harder to flatten without splitting.
 * *Pros:* Extremely unique and eye-catching; great for a small accent piece.
 * *Cons:* Highest risk of cracking, requires the most careful handling; may not have enough wood for a full desk top if you're very limited.

**My Recommendation:** Go with **face-grain**. It offers the best balance of visual appeal and workability for your situation. You can glue up multiple slabs to create a wide panel that showcases the unique grain patterns of each piece.

### 4. Drying & Finishing Tips

*   **Slow Dry:** Blueberry wood is prone to cracking, so dry it slowly in a well-ventilated area (ideally 10-20% relative humidity).
*   **Seal First:** Apply a thin coat of oil or wax to the raw wood before gluing to help prevent moisture loss during drying.
*   **Finish Last:** Once assembled, apply your final finish (oil, polyurethane, etc.) after all glue has cured and the piece is fully dry.

By embracing the unique characteristics of blueberry wood and choosing a design that works with its limitations, you can create a beautiful and functional desk top. Good luck with your project!
