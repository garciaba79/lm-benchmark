#### 2. Milling Strategy

The goal is to maximize yield while managing the difficulty of shaping small pieces.

*   **Bandsaw Rig Assessment:** A 10" bandsaw rig for ripping round wood sounds ambitious, especially with the potential for warping in a hard material. Test your setup thoroughly before committing large amounts of time to milling.
*   **Alternative Milling Techniques (If Bandsaw Fails):** If ripping is too difficult, consider if you can use hand tools or specialized jig systems that work on curves or irregular shapes, even if it’s slower. For the small, irregular pieces, careful assessment of how much material you can salvage might be necessary over achieving perfect straights.
*   **Waste Minimization:** Since glue-ups will create waste, plan your cuts based on the final desired dimensions *before* milling or gluing to minimize offcuts.

#### 3. Desk Design and Material Use

Given the limited supply and desire for minimalism:

*   **Focus on Panel Size:** Design your pieces around maximizing the use of the largest usable boards first. Since you are glue-ups, think about how the resulting panels will look when joined to minimize visible seams (e.g., using wide planks for the top surface).

#### 4. Grain Orientation Decision

The choice of grain orientation will fundamentally dictate the aesthetic feel of the desk:

*   **Face-Grain (Recommended for Aesthetic Focus):** Running the grain parallel to the primary visual line (the long axis of the desk top). This emphasizes the unique, fine grain patterns you observed in the wood and often looks the most natural and elegant for a minimalist piece.
    *   *Consideration:* If the wood is heavily figured or has prominent knots, face-grain highlights these features.
*   **Edge-Grain:** Showing the side of the board. This can create a very linear, architectural look. It might be less visually engaging if the grain pattern isn't highly attractive.
*   **End-Grain:** Showing the cross-section of the wood. This emphasizes the growth rings and the density of the material. In thick, hard wood like blueberry, this can provide a very dramatic, textural aesthetic, emphasizing the hardness and density.

**Recommendation:** For a minimalist design focused on natural beauty, **face-grain** is usually the most aesthetically pleasing choice, allowing the subtle patterns to define the surface without unnecessary visual distraction from joints or grain direction changes.
