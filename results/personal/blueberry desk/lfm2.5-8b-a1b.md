**Grain‑orientation options for the desk top**

1. **Face grain (smooth surface visible)** – Gives a clean, even look but requires that the board be cut so the growth rings run parallel to the tabletop edge. This works well if you can plane or joint each small piece after ripping.

2. **Edge grain (side of the board showing the growth rings)** – Provides a pronounced, linear pattern; ideal for a rustic aesthetic. Cut boards lengthwise and then rip them flat so the long edges are on the face.

3. **End grain (ends of a log or branch)** – Exposes the natural “spoke” figure; best for decorative accents but can be more difficult to plane evenly because of irregular fibers.

**Milling small pieces without bends**

- **Pre‑ripping with a fine‑tooth blade**: Set the bandsaw for thin cuts (≈ 1/8″) using a high‑speed steel blade. This reduces tear-out on the delicate grain.
- **Support jig or sled**: Clamp each piece to a sacrificial MDF backing that matches its thickness; the rig will keep it flat while you cut, preventing cupping.
- **Progressive planing**: After an initial ripping pass, run the board through a hand plane or a low‑speed router table with a straight bit to flatten any residual twist before final saw cuts.
- **Batch gluing**: While one strip is still slightly uneven, glue it to adjacent strips on a workbench using a spread of epoxy. The shared surface forces the pieces to stay aligned and share moisture evenly.
