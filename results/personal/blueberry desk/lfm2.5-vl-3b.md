**Milling Tips for Small Pieces (1-3" Thick)**  
- **Use a fine-toothed blade:** A 12‑tooth or finer blade will cut cleaner and reduce the chance of warping.  
- **Set the rip speed low:** Start at about 30–40 RPM for hardwood; lower speeds give better control on thin sections.  
- **Keep the feed rate steady:** Use a push stick or a handheld guide to maintain consistent pressure, especially when the wood is uneven.  
- **Check for binding early:** If the blade starts to bind, stop and adjust the depth of cut or change the angle slightly.  
- **Consider a small‑diameter saw (e.g., 1/4" or 3/8")** for very thin pieces; it can produce straighter cuts with less effort.

**Design Solutions for Limited Wood**  
- **Optimize leg placement:** Place legs at opposite corners of the desk top to maximize usable surface area while keeping the footprint small.  
- **Use a single‑piece apron:** A continuous apron that spans the entire width reduces waste and simplifies assembly.  
- **Glue up with minimal cuts:** Instead of cutting many pieces, glue together larger blocks (e.g., two 2×4s) to form the top surface; this saves material and reduces offcuts.  
- **Incorporate a “top‑of‑the‑box” design:** Build the desk as a solid slab with a removable drawer front or a simple sliding panel, which can be added later if you have extra wood.

**Grain Orientation Choices & Practical Tips**  
1. **Face‑grain (long grain running lengthwise):**  
   - Shows the natural blueberry texture and color variation.  
   - Best for a minimalist look; it highlights the wood’s character without needing to hide edges.  

2. **Edge‑grain:**  
   - Provides a clean, uniform surface that can be painted or stained evenly.  
   - Works well if you want a sleek, modern appearance and are comfortable with occasional edge cuts.  

3. **End‑grain (short grain):**  
   - Gives a subtle, almost “smooth” look; it’s less common for desks but can add a unique visual interest.  
   - Requires careful handling to avoid splitting; use a low‑speed saw and keep the piece flat on the workbench.

**Additional Considerations**  
- **Finish first:** Apply a light oil or wax before final assembly to protect the wood and bring out its grain.  
- **Reinforce joints:** Use pocket holes or dowels for leg attachments if you need extra strength, especially with thin pieces.  
- **Plan for future expansion:** Leave a small gap (about ¼") between the apron and the desk top so you can add drawers later without cutting into existing material.
