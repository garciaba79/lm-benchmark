### **1. Drying Blueberry Wood**
- **Air-Dry Method**:
  - Stack pieces in a dry, well-ventilated area (e.g., barn, shed) with cross-bracing to prevent warping.
  - Use **kiln-dry** if available—blueberry wood dries slowly but can crack if moisture fluctuates rapidly. Aim for **12–18% moisture content**.
  - Split logs into smaller sections (3–4 ft long) before drying to speed up drying and reduce stress.

- **Crack Prevention**:
  - Avoid stacking pieces directly on the ground; use pallets or supports.
  - Use **moisture meters** to check progress—stop drying when moisture is stable (~12%).
  - If cracks appear, sand lightly (320-grit) and re-glue if needed.

---

### **2. Milling Challenges & Solutions**
#### **Bandsaw Rig for Round Wood**:
- **Setup**:
  - Use a **jig with adjustable stops** to guide round wood into the saw’s throat plate.
  - For irregular shapes, clamp pieces securely (e.g., with **clamp-on guides**) to prevent drift.
  - Test cuts on scrap wood first to adjust blade pressure and feed rate.

#### **Small Pieces (1–3" Thick)**:
- **Problem**: Warping/curling due to uneven drying or moisture content.
- **Solutions**:
  - Dry pieces individually for **6+ months** before milling to reduce warping risk.
  - Use a **dehumidifier** in the drying area to control humidity (~30–50%).
  - For edge-gluing, pre-drill holes (1/8" diameter) to prevent splitting.

#### **Time-Saving Tips**:
- Group similar-sized pieces for batch milling.
- Pre-cut larger blanks into smaller segments if needed (e.g., rip into strips for apron boards).

---

### **3. Desk Design Constraints**
- **Minimalist Aesthetic**:
