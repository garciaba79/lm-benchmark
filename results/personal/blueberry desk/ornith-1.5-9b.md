This is a great project with some real tradeoffs baked in. Here are some thoughts on each area, plus a couple of questions that might help you move forward.

**Blueberry wood characteristics**
- Its density and hardness mean sharp tools are essential—dull blades will tear out the fine grain rather than cut it cleanly. A fresh blade and slower feed rate on the bandsaw will help.
- Because it cracks when improperly dried, drying is your most critical step. Dry slowly (clamped boards or a band), keep humidity changes gradual, and let it fully acclimate before finishing—rushing this is what causes movement and checks.

**Milling / the bandsaw rig**
- A sled-style jig for ripping rounds is worth building; it keeps the fence parallel to the blade so you get flatter faces than a hand-fed round stock will allow.
- Since milling will be slow, batch your cuts by thickness first (get all pieces to a rough target), then refine—this reduces re-measuring and setup time.

**Design**
- A minimalist design is the right call given limited material; it also makes glue-ups more forgiving since you're not cutting complex joinery from short stock.
- Plan your glue-up layout on paper first, since waste here directly eats into your supply. Consider using offcuts for smaller parts (legs, apron) rather than only for dimensional stock.

**Grain orientation (the key decision)**
This is where your limited wood supply really matters:
- **Face-grain:** Shows the most figure and blueberry's fine grain—best aesthetically—but uses the widest portion of each board and wastes the most material on the top.
- **Edge-grain:** More economical; you still get layered texture while using less width per board, leaving more for other parts.
- **End-grain:** Shows growth rings but looks very busy and consumes a lot of stock—usually not ideal for a large desk top.

Given your constraints, I'd lean toward edge-grain on the top if you want to preserve supply, or face-grain only if showing off the grain is the priority over material conservation.

1. What's driving your wood shortage—limited total board feet, or specifically narrow/short pieces? That changes how aggressive you can be with glue-ups and layout.
2. Is the desk top going to be a single wide panel (favoring face-grain) or built from narrower boards (favoring edge-grain)?
