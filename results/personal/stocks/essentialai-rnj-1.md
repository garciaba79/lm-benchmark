> #### 🤖 Assistant

Options trading is like an advanced version of buying and selling stocks, but with some key differences that can be thought of as "insurance" or "bets" on future stock prices.

Here's a simple explanation:

Imagine you own a car (stock) worth $30,000. You're worried about it losing value if the economy goes down. So you buy insurance (options contract) for $500 that covers 90% of your car's value. Now even if your car drops to $20,000, you'll still get $18,000 back.

In options trading:
- You pay a premium (like insurance cost)
- You have the right but not obligation to buy/sell stock at a set price
- Options expire on specific dates

There are two main types:
1. Call options: Bet the stock will go up
2. Put options: Bet the stock will go down

The key difference from owning stocks is that with options, you can control 100 shares of stock for just the premium paid (like controlling a car worth $30k for just $500 in insurance). This allows you to take bigger bets with less money.

Like with stocks, options have risks and rewards. They're more complex than buying/selling stocks directly, but offer ways to protect investments or speculate on price movements with different risk/reward profiles.
