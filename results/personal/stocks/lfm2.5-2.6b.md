# Options Trading Explained Simply

## The Basic Idea

When you **buy a stock**, you own an actual share of the company and can benefit from its price increase.

**Options** give you the *right* (but not the obligation) to buy or sell a stock at a specific price by a certain date. They're like insurance policies on stocks.

## Two Main Types

### 1. **Call Options** (Right to Buy)
- You pay a fee called a **premium** for the right to *buy* a stock
- Lets you profit if the stock goes up, but not if it goes down
- **Example:** Stock is $100, your call has a strike price of $110. If the stock rises above $110 by expiration, you can buy at $110 and sell at the higher market price.

### 2. **Put Options** (Right to Sell)
- You pay a premium for the right to *sell* a stock
- Lets you profit if the stock goes down, but not if it goes up
- **Example:** Stock is $100, your put has a strike price of $90. If the stock falls below $90 by expiration, you can sell at $90 and lock in that value.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Strike Price** | The price at which you can buy (call) or sell (put) |
| **Expiration Date** | When your option becomes worthless if not exercised |
| **Premium** | The cost of buying the option |
| **In-the-Money** | The option's intrinsic value is positive |

## Simple Strategies

1. **Buy a Call + Sell Covered Call**: Buying a call while owning stock (or selling calls) to generate income
2. **Selling Calls**: Collect premium for betting the stock won't rise much, then only profit if it does significantly
3. **Straddles/Stripes**: Betting on volatility regardless of direction

## Risks & Rewards

- **Limited risk** (you can't lose more than your premium paid)
- **Asymmetric payoff** - profits are capped at strike price, but losses are limited to the premium
- Requires understanding time decay (theta) and volatility (vega)

The key difference from stocks: you're paying for *potential upside* rather than owning the actual asset.
