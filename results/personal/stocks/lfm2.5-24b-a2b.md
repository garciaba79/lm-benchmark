> #### 💬 User

Explain options trading in simple terms if I'm familiar with buying and selling stocks.

> #### 🤖 Assistant

Think of an option as a ticket to a future trade. You buy or sell a contract that gives you the *right*, but not the obligation, to buy (call) or sell (put) 100 shares of a stock at a set price by a certain date.

**Call option:** You're betting the stock will go up. If it does, you can exercise your ticket and buy the stock at the agreed price (usually lower than the current market), keeping the difference as profit. You only pay the cost of the ticket itself — so leverage is built in.

**Put option:** You're protecting against a drop or betting on a decline. At expiration, if the stock has fallen below your strike price, you can sell it at that higher locked-in price, limiting your loss.

Since options aren't stocks, they have an expiration date and the contract can expire worthless if the stock doesn't move in your favor — so you could lose the entire ticket cost. Conversely, a call or put can gain significant value if the underlying moves far enough. It's like owning a stock but with a time limit and borrowed capital from the option seller.

In short: you're trading on expectations about direction and timing, not necessarily ownership of the company itself — which is why it's often described as "insurance" or "leverage."
