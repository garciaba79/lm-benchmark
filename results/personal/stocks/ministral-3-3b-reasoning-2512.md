Sure, let me explain options trading in simple terms for someone who is familiar with buying and selling stocks.

In options trading, you buy or sell a contract that gives you the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an asset at a specific price on or before a certain date.

- **Call Option**: You buy a call option, which gives you the right to buy an asset (like a stock) at a set price (the strike price) by a certain date. If the stock price goes up, your option is more valuable; if it goes down, it becomes worthless.
- **Put Option**: You buy a put option, which gives you the right to sell an asset at a set price by a certain date. If the stock price goes down, your option is more valuable; if it goes up, it becomes worthless.

When you trade options, you are not required to actually buy or sell the underlying asset. You can use the profits from the option's movement without owning the asset itself.

Key terms:
- **Strike Price**: The price at which the option can be exercised.
- **Expiration Date**: The date by which the option must be exercised or lost.
- **Premium**: The price you pay to buy an option. It is the cost of the right, not the value of the asset.

Options trading involves risk and can be more complex than buying and selling stocks directly. Always make sure you understand the risks before investing in options.
