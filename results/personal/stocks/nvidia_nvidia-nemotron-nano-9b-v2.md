Explain options trading in simple terms if I'm familiar with buying and selling stocks.

> #### 🤖 Assistant

Options trading is a way to bet on whether a stock’s price will go up or down without owning the stock itself. Here’s how it works in simple terms:

1. **Calls vs. Puts**:  
   - A **call option** is like betting the stock price will rise. You pay a fee (called a "premium") for the right to buy the stock at a set price (the "strike price") before a deadline (expiration date). If the stock hits or exceeds that price, you profit by buying it cheaply and selling it at the higher market price.  
   - A **put option** is the opposite: you bet the stock will fall. You pay a premium for the right to sell the stock at the strike price. If the stock drops below that price, you profit by selling it at the higher strike price instead of the lower market price.

2. **Key Terms**:  
   - **Strike Price**: The fixed price in the contract.  
   - **Premium**: The cost to buy the option (like an upfront bet).  
   - **Expiration Date**: When the option "expires" if not used.

3. **Risks & Rewards**:  
   - If your prediction is wrong, you lose only the premium paid (not more).  
   - If correct, profits can be larger than buying stocks outright because options require less money upfront (leverage).  

Example: If you think Apple stock will rise from $150 to $170, you could buy a call option with a $160 strike price. If Apple hits $170, you exercise the option (buy at $160) and sell at $170 for a $10 profit per share (minus the premium paid). If it stays below $160, you lose only the premium.
