No, I don't think "React is dead" is accurate — though there's genuine competition worth taking seriously.

**Where the premise has merit:** SvelteKit, SolidStart, and Qwik are legitimate projects that have gained real traction. They offer compelling advantages: smaller bundle sizes (Svelte/Solid compile away much of the virtual DOM), strong perceived performance, and fresh architectural ideas like resumability (Qwik). For new greenfield projects, teams sometimes make a reasoned choice toward these alternatives based on their specific needs.

**Why "dead" is an overstatement:** Frameworks compete on different axes — performance, bundle size, developer experience, ecosystem — and React remains dominant rather than obsolete. The more accurate framing is that the frontend ecosystem has *matured and diversified*, giving teams more options.

**What React still brings to real-world production today:**

- **Ecosystem maturity:** Vast libraries of third-party packages, components, and tooling built specifically around React, reducing integration friction.
- **Community and talent pool:** Millions of developers are familiar with React, making hiring and onboarding significantly easier for teams.
- **Meta's continued investment:** Active development, regular releases, and a large engineering team behind it (unlike some smaller projects).
- **Enterprise adoption at scale:** Many major companies run React in production across millions of users, which validates its stability and reliability.
- **React Native:** Extends the same skills to mobile development, broadening its value proposition.
- **Stability and documentation:** Well-trodden patterns lower risk for large teams adopting or maintaining it long-term.

The reality is that React isn't dying — it's competing in a more mature market where alternatives genuinely offer tradeoffs worth considering. The best choice depends on the project, team, and priorities rather than any framework being universally "dead."
